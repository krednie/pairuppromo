# PairUp — Pre-Launch Website Story

## Core idea

PairUp is not presented as another networking platform.

It is presented as the place where unfinished ideas finally meet the people needed to build them.

The website begins with isolation.

One person has an idea.
Another person can design.
Someone else can code.
Another person knows how to pitch.

They are all capable, but they are scattered.

As the visitor scrolls, these people slowly discover one another. Individual profiles, skills, and ideas begin moving closer together. Separate signals become a team. A team becomes momentum.

The page ends by inviting the visitor to become part of that first circle.

The emotional journey is:

**Alone → Discovered → Connected → Building → Belonging**

---

# Chapter 1 — The empty space

## What appears

The page opens with a dark, spacious screen.

There is very little visible at first.

In the centre:

**Great ideas deserve the right team.**

Below it, after a small pause:

**Build better. Together.**

The PairUp name appears subtly above or beside the headline.

The background contains faint moving points. They look like distant people or signals, but they are not yet connected.

A small line appears under the headline:

**Find people from your university who are ready to build something real.**

The primary button:

**Join the founding circle**

A small secondary line:

**Early members get a permanent Founding Builder badge, referral perks, and priority access.**

## How it feels

The first screen should feel cinematic, quiet, and slightly mysterious.

Not empty in a boring way.

Empty in a way that creates anticipation.

The user should feel that something is about to happen.

## Motion

The headline enters slowly, not with a standard fade.

Letters can appear with slight blur and sharpen into focus.

The background dots drift independently.

When the user moves the mouse, some dots subtly move toward the pointer, suggesting attraction and connection.

The CTA should have a soft magnetic effect when hovered.

---

# Chapter 2 — Everyone is looking for someone

## What appears

As the user scrolls, the large headline moves upward and individual floating statements begin appearing across the screen.

Examples:

**“I have the idea. I need someone who can build it.”**

**“I can code, but I need a designer.”**

**“I want to join hackathons, but I never find the right team.”**

**“I have skills. I just don’t know the right people.”**

**“I’m ready to build something beyond assignments.”**

These statements should appear as messages, thoughts, or floating cards.

Each one belongs to a different type of student.

Small skill labels may appear around them:

`React`
`UI/UX`
`AI/ML`
`Pitching`
`Product`
`Research`
`Video`
`Hardware`

## Story purpose

This section tells the visitor:

**You are not the only one struggling to find the right people.**

It turns a personal frustration into a shared problem.

It should feel like hundreds of students are sending signals into the same space without knowing the others exist.

## Motion

Cards enter from different edges.

They move independently at first.

As the user continues scrolling, the distance between them becomes smaller.

Thin lines briefly appear between cards with complementary skills.

For example:

`Idea` connects to `Developer`.

`Developer` connects to `Designer`.

`Designer` connects to `Presenter`.

The connections should feel organic, not like a technical network diagram.

---

# Chapter 3 — The collision

## What appears

The scattered messages begin converging toward the centre of the screen.

The background becomes more energetic.

The central message:

**Your next great team may already be here.**

Alternative supporting line:

**The people you need are closer than you think.**

Several profile cards start forming a cluster.

A team may visually consist of:

* One developer
* One designer
* One product thinker
* One presenter

The profiles should not look like formal resumes.

They should feel expressive and fast.

Each card may show:

* First name
* University
* Main skill
* What they want to build
* Availability status
* A small personality signal

Example:

**Aarav**
Developer
Interested in AI tools and hackathons
`Ready to build`

**Meera**
Designer
Interested in consumer apps
`Looking for a team`

## Story purpose

This is the first moment where PairUp becomes the answer.

The earlier sections showed scattered potential.

This section shows what happens when the right people collide.

The promise is not just connection.

The promise is momentum.

## Motion

The individual cards move into orbit around a central glowing point.

The centre may contain the PairUp symbol.

When the cards align, a quick visual pulse travels through the network.

The words may change from:

**Searching**

to

**Matched**

to

**Building**

This should be one of the most satisfying moments on the page.

---

# Chapter 4 — The place is already moving

## What appears

The next section should make PairUp feel alive.

Not like an empty platform waiting for users.

The heading:

**Something is already forming.**

Or:

**Builders are already showing up.**

Below it, there is a live-looking stream of activity.

Examples:

**A designer joined from Jaipur.**

**Three AI builders are looking for a fourth teammate.**

**A product idea in sustainability is looking for a developer.**

**Someone reserved the username “pixelpilot.”**

**Five new builders joined today.**

The section can contain animated numbers:

**248 early builders**

**37 active ideas**

**12 universities represented**

**68 teams forming**

These numbers can later be connected to real data.

During pre-launch, they can be controlled from one configuration file so they are easy to update.

## Story purpose

This section removes the fear that the platform will feel dead.

It tells the visitor:

**Other ambitious people are already here.**

The user should feel that they are arriving early, but not arriving alone.

## Visual style

This section can look like a large living dashboard, but not a traditional analytics dashboard.

It should feel more like signals passing through a digital city.

Activity cards can appear, disappear, and rearrange.

Small avatar circles can move through the interface.

Skill tags may continuously flow across the screen.

## Motion

Numbers count upward when the section enters.

Activity messages slide in gently and move into a queue.

Profile circles light up one by one.

A thin line may travel between different university labels.

The movement should suggest that PairUp continues operating even when the visitor is not interacting with it.

---

# Chapter 5 — Build anything

## What appears

This section broadens the product beyond hackathons.

The heading:

**Not every great team begins with a competition.**

Then:

**Build the startup. Ship the side project. Enter the hackathon. Launch the club. Make the film. Start the research.**

Each possibility appears as a visual world.

Examples:

### Hackathons

Find complementary skills before registration closes.

### Startups

Meet people who want to build beyond the idea stage.

### Side projects

Turn saved notes and unfinished prototypes into something real.

### Research

Find collaborators with different technical and academic strengths.

### Creative work

Form teams for films, design projects, content, or campus initiatives.

## Story purpose

This section changes the visitor’s mental model.

PairUp is not only a hackathon team finder.

It is a campus collaboration layer.

The central message:

**Whatever you want to make, you should not have to build it alone.**

## Motion

The website can move through different visual environments as the user scrolls.

A hackathon timer transforms into a product screen.

The product screen transforms into a research board.

The research board transforms into a film frame.

All of them eventually collapse into the same shape:

A group of people building together.

---

# Chapter 6 — The Founding Circle

## What appears

The page becomes calmer again.

A premium badge appears in the centre.

It should not look like a cheap gamification icon.

It should feel like membership in the first generation of the product.

Title:

**Founding Builder**

Supporting copy:

**For the people who joined before PairUp became obvious.**

Perks appear beneath it:

* Permanent Founding Builder badge
* Priority access when matching opens
* Early profile visibility
* Reserved username
* Access to the founding community
* Voting access for early features
* Referral unlocks
* Invitations to private team-forming sessions

Small line:

**Available only during the pre-launch phase.**

## Story purpose

The badge is not merely a reward.

It represents identity, status, and being early.

The emotional trigger is:

**I was part of this before everyone else joined.**

## Visual style

The badge can have a metallic, iridescent, or glass-like surface.

It may rotate very slowly.

The user’s cursor can affect the lighting.

Around it, tiny names or initials of early members can appear, suggesting that the founding circle is already forming.

---

# Chapter 7 — Referral loop

## What appears

The next section explains the referral system without making it feel transactional.

Heading:

**The best teams usually begin with one introduction.**

Then:

**Join PairUp, receive your personal link, and invite people you would want in the network.**

The perks can unlock progressively.

Example:

### Join

Founding Builder badge

### 2 verified referrals

Priority access

### 5 verified referrals

Profile boost at launch

### 10 verified referrals

Community Builder badge

### 20 verified referrals

Founding ambassador access

The wording should say **verified referrals**, not only referrals, so low-quality spam is discouraged.

## Story purpose

This converts every signup into distribution.

It also reinforces the idea that PairUp grows through trusted university networks.

## Motion

A referral link appears and branches outward.

Each referral adds another node.

As the nodes grow, new perks visually unlock.

The experience should resemble a network expanding rather than a progress bar filling.

---

# Chapter 8 — The final invitation

## What appears

The final screen is simple and direct.

The earlier motion slows down.

All the scattered people and signals from the page are now connected in the background.

Main line:

**Your next team starts with one signup.**

Alternative:

**The right people are showing up. Be one of them.**

The input is placed in the centre.

Users initially see only one field.

Possible default:

**University email**

or

**Phone number**

The user should not be shown a large form immediately.

After entering the primary detail, a few optional pills appear:

**What do you bring?**

`Development`
`Design`
`AI/ML`
`Product`
`Research`
`Pitching`
`Content`
`Hardware`
`Still exploring`

Then:

**What are you looking to build?**

`Hackathon team`
`Startup`
`Side project`
`Research`
`Creative project`
`Anything interesting`

The final button:

**Claim my Founding Builder badge**

Under the button:

**Free to join. Takes less than 20 seconds.**

## After signup

The form transforms into a success state.

Message:

**You’re in. Welcome to the founding circle.**

The user sees:

* Their badge preview
* Their personal referral link
* Current referral count
* Next unlock
* A share button
* A copy-link button

Example:

**Invite 2 verified builders to unlock priority access.**

The signup should feel like entering something, not submitting a form.

# Visual direction

## Overall mood

The site should feel:

* Cinematic
* Youthful
* Intelligent
* Premium
* Social
* Energetic
* Slightly futuristic

It should not feel:

* Corporate
* Like a college portal
* Like a generic SaaS template
* Overly playful
* Like a crypto project
* Like a traditional networking website

## Colour palette

### Base

Deep black-blue:

`#070912`

This creates depth without using pure black.

### Primary

Electric violet:

`#7B61FF`

Represents creativity, technology, and ambition.

### Human accent

Warm coral:

`#FF6B6B`

Used for moments involving people, action, or energy.

### Secondary glow

Electric cyan:

`#5DE4FF`

Used for signals, connections, and live activity.

### Text

Primary text:

`#F7F7FB`

Secondary text:

`#A7A9B8`

### Badge

The Founding Builder badge can combine violet, cyan, silver, and a slight warm highlight.

---

# Typography

The typography should be large and confident.

Headlines should feel editorial, not like software documentation.

Suggested structure:

* Very large display font for major statements
* Clean sans-serif for paragraphs and interface elements
* Monospace used only for small live signals, referral codes, and status labels

Headlines should be short.

Examples:

**Build better. Together.**

**Everyone is looking for someone.**

**Something is already forming.**

**Build anything.**

**Join the founding circle.**

---

# Motion language

Motion should tell the story.

It should not exist only to impress.

## Main motion principles

### Separation

At the beginning, objects move independently.

### Attraction

As the user scrolls, complementary elements begin pulling toward one another.

### Connection

Lines, pulses, and orbiting movement represent discovery.

### Acceleration

The middle of the page becomes faster and more energetic as teams form.

### Belonging

Near the end, motion becomes stable and coordinated.

Everything that once moved separately now moves as one system.

---

# GSAP implementation approach

GSAP would control the major scroll-based story.

## ScrollTrigger

Each chapter becomes a scroll scene.

Some sections stay pinned while the animation progresses.

For example:

* Hero stays fixed while isolated dots appear
* Profile cards converge while the user scrolls
* Numbers increase when the activity section enters
* The Founding Builder badge rotates and reveals perks
* The final network settles behind the signup form

## Timelines

Each chapter uses one timeline.

This prevents the page from feeling like unrelated animations.

The timeline controls:

* Text entrances
* Card movement
* Blur changes
* Scale
* Opacity
* Connection lines
* Background lighting
* Section transitions

## Mouse interactions

GSAP can create:

* Magnetic buttons
* Profile cards that tilt slightly
* Badge lighting that follows the cursor
* Background particles attracted to the pointer
* Subtle movement inside skill pills

## Text animation

SplitText-style animations can reveal headlines word by word or line by line.

The effect should be smooth and restrained.

Important words such as:

**ideas**
**team**
**together**
**building**

can be emphasised differently.

# Technical structure

The final site can be built with:

* React
* Vite
* GSAP
* GSAP ScrollTrigger
* Framer Motion for small interface transitions
* Lenis for smooth scrolling
* CSS variables for the visual system
* Supabase or Firebase later for lead storage
* A lightweight API for referral creation

GSAP should handle cinematic scroll scenes.

Framer Motion should handle small UI states such as:

* Form step changes
* Pill selection
* Success confirmation
* Referral unlock animations

They should not compete with one another.

# Page structure

The page will contain:

1. Hero
2. Scattered builder signals
3. Team collision
4. Live activity and social momentum
5. Build-anything possibilities
6. Founding Builder badge
7. Referral perks
8. Final signup
9. Signup success and referral state

There does not need to be a traditional navbar.

A minimal floating top bar can contain:

**PairUp**

and

**Join early**

The top bar can become visible after the hero.

# Copy direction

The language should be short, confident, and human.

Avoid:

**Connect with like-minded individuals.**

Use:

**Meet people who want to build.**

Avoid:

**Create your profile and explore collaboration opportunities.**

Use:

**Show what you bring. Find what you’re missing.**

Avoid:

**Join our growing community.**

Use:

**The first builders are already here.**

Avoid:

**Get early access.**

Use:

**Join the founding circle.**

---

# Final emotional outcome

By the time the visitor reaches the signup, they should feel:

* I am not the only person searching.
* There are talented people around me.
* This could help me finally start something.
* The network already feels active.
* Joining early gives me status and access.
* Signing up takes almost no effort.
* I want to see who else is here.

The website should not simply explain PairUp.

It should make the visitor briefly experience what PairUp promises:

**The feeling of finding your people.**
